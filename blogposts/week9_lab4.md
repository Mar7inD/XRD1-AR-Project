## Introduction

For my VR project, I decided to create an Escape Room game that takes advantage of virtual reality’s immersive capabilities. The game is about a player trapped in two connected rooms. The atmosphere will be sci-fi. The goal is to uncover and interpret various clues to solve a puzzle and unlock a keypad on the servers before they overheat and melt down. The player must find the correct code within the given time limit to prevent the system from imploding.

## Progress

My first step was to brainstorm different clue ideas and think about how I could use VR’s interactivity to make the experience feel more special—something more engaging than a regular computer game, but also way cheaper than building a real Escape Room.

The first clue I came up with was a UV flashlight. When the player shines it on certain surfaces, hidden text appears and leads them toward solving the puzzle.

While thinking of the second clue, I remembered something Kasper mentioned in one of his presentations. He showed an AR example with a really expensive machine that, if broken in real life, costs a lot to fix—but in AR you can just reset it with one click.

That sparked the idea for my second clue: a set of breakable boxes. The player needs to smash them to find a key, which then opens a chest containing the next clue.
